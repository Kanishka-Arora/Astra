TACTICAL INTELLIGENCE PROTOCOLS

Safety Assistant

Instant situational action plans designed to help you de-escalate, deter potential threats, maintain spatial awareness, and take decisive protective action.

In Immediate Imminent Danger?

Do not read guides. Initiate immediate defense protocols below.

1. Feeling Unsafe While Walking Alone / At Night
▼

When walking alone, attackers target unawareness and isolated pathways. Maintain the tactical initiative:

  • Remove earphones: Do not wear headphones or look down at your screen while walking. Keep auditory awareness sharp.
  • Walk near the curb: Avoid walking closely alongside dark alleys, doorways, or blind corners.
  • Project confidence: Walk at a brisk, purposeful pace with your head up and eyes sweeping the perimeter.
  • Fake phone call / Live location: Send a live location link to a trusted contact or hold a deliberate, audible voice call stating your current street and estimated arrival time.
  • Identify open businesses: Keep track of 24/7 pharmacies, open petrol stations, or convenience stores you can quickly divert into.
2. Travelling Alone (Cab, Rideshare, or Public Transit)
▼

Rideshares and private cabs introduce confined spaces. Enforce these safety checks prior to and during your trip:

  • Verify vehicle registration and driver identity: Check the plate number and driver profile before entering. Never get into a car that does not match the app.
  • Sit in the back seat behind the driver: This makes it impossible for the driver to reach you without fully turning around and leaves you access to both rear doors.
  • Test child-lock before leaving curb: Open the door slightly to ensure you are not locked from the inside.
  • Share your ride trip link: Broadcast your live ride link to family or emergency contacts through Astra or your rideshare app.
  • Immediate route divergence action: If the vehicle takes an unexplained turn or highway detour, verbally question the driver immediately and loudly. If answers are evasive, prepare the SOS trigger.
3. Lost or Stranded in an Unfamiliar Area
▼

When disoriented in unfamiliar territory, prevent panic and stabilize your position:

  • Do not look visibly lost: Stop inside an illuminated establishment (coffee shop, store, hotel lobby) rather than standing in the middle of a dark street staring at your phone.
  • Use Astra Geolocation: Open Astra's Dashboard, hit "Get Current Location", and copy your coordinates to send to a guardian or taxi service.
  • Conserve mobile battery: Switch your phone to Low Power Mode and disable unnecessary background apps.
  • Call an official taxi or trusted contact: Avoid accepting unmarked car rides or unsolicited offers from strangers on the road.
4. Facing Harassment or Suspected Stalker
▼

If you suspect someone is following you or persistent harassment begins:

  • Conduct the 4-Right-Turns Test: If walking or driving, make 4 consecutive right turns. If the individual is still behind you, they are undeniably following you.
  • Cross the street: Cross to the opposite sidewalk. Look back casually to confirm if their path alters to match yours.
  • Do NOT head directly home: Do not lead a stalker to your residence or apartment gate. Head to the nearest police station, hospital, or crowded venue.
  • Make noise and draw witnesses: Look directly at the stalker to eliminate anonymity. In public, speak clearly and loudly: "Stop following me!" to alert bystanders.
  • Document in Astra: Log a report with date, time, description, and location once you reach safety.
5. Imminent Physical Confrontation / Crisis
▼

When physical safety is compromised and an altercation is immediate:

  • Create distance first: Run towards light, traffic, or populated zones. Physical distance is the highest-probability survival tactic.
  • Trigger the Astra Siren: Activate the Acoustic Safety Siren on full volume. The loud disorienting high-pitch frequency breaks surprise and draws immediate public eyes.
  • Target vulnerable anatomy: If forced to defend, strike high-leverage areas: eyes, nose bridge, throat, groin, or knee joints. Use personal items (keys, heels, bag corners).
  • Shout "FIRE" instead of "HELP": Studies demonstrate that shouting "FIRE" draws significantly faster citizen emergence than "HELP".
  • Dial 112 / 100: Keep lines open even if you drop the phone so dispatchers can hear and trace background audio.
